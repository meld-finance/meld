Meld started because no single app answered everyday questions and long-term ones from the same, complete set of accounts—with automatic syncing instead of time-consuming, one-time CSV imports.

Retirement planners

ProjectionLab and Boldin are great at the long view. But balances are typed in by hand, imported via one-time CSVs, or synced only on a paid plan, and once the plan is set they can't tell you what you spent last month or which bills just went up.

Budgeting apps

Monarch, YNAB and Copilot link your banks and track spending, for a yearly subscription. Retirement, taxes, insurance and estate planning mostly stay in a spreadsheet somewhere else.

AI assistants

ChatGPT and Claude can now link bank accounts too. That's yet another place to connect everything, and it sees what you link, not your home, insurance policies, tax estimate or retirement plan.

Self-hosted apps

Actual and Firefly III are free and keep your data yours. They're mostly about budgeting, though, and getting bank data in usually means manual CSV imports or running a Docker server and paying for SimpleFIN.

Automatic bank syncing—no tedious CSV imports. Link your accounts once and Meld keeps balances, holdings and transactions current automatically. The same live data answers this week's questions (where the money went, what's recurring, will autopay clear) and the big ones (am I on track, what will I owe, is my estate in order), without exporting spreadsheets or pasting numbers in.

The trade-offs

You set up your own free Plaid developer account before connecting real banks—Plaid should approve production access automatically for personal use. There's no app in the App Store yet: it's a Mac app you reach from your phone over a private network, or a Docker server you run. It's built for one household.

Why it's worth it

Your data sits in a database on your own computer—there's no central service that stores your financial history, and no account with a vendor that can shut down or get breached. You use your own Plaid keys to connect directly to your banks.

What it really costs

Meld itself is free. The only bills are for services you choose to use, paid directly to them, with no markup and no middleman.

Plaid's free Trial plan is for Plaid teams created after April 15, 2026; older teams can use Limited Production. Plaid sets its own prices; our Plaid setup guide walks through it.

Get started

Try it with Plaid's test bank in a few minutes, then connect your real accounts once Plaid approves your keys (usually automatically).

Download Meld

Get the Apple silicon or Intel Mac app and drag it to Applications. It’s signed and notarized by Apple. On Windows, run the installer (preview).

Set it up

Choose a passphrase and paste your Plaid keys. Turn on Ask Meld with free local Ollama models, your ChatGPT plan, or a Claude API key (optional).

Try Sandbox, then go live

Look around with test data (user_good / pass_good), then connect your real accounts.

Use Meld on your phone

Check balances, upcoming autopays or Ask Meld from your phone without opening Meld to the internet. Your phone reaches your computer over Tailscale, a free private network only your devices are on.

  1. Install Tailscale on both your Mac and your phone, and sign in to both with the same account.
  2. Turn on HTTPS certificates in the Tailscale admin console, then click Turn on phone access in Meld's Settings.
  3. Scan the QR code with your phone and use Add to Home Screen so Meld opens like an app.
