Privacy: where your data goes

Every outside service Meld talks to, what is sent, how digit masking works, and what is encrypted on disk.

5 min read View PRIVACY.md on GitHub ↗
On this page
What's protected on disk Reporting a security problem

Meld runs on your own computer. There is no Meld server, no account with the author, no analytics and no telemetry. Your data lives in the Postgres database and the documents folder on your machine: with the Mac app, both are in ~/Library/Application Support/Meld (%APPDATA%\Meld on Windows), and its Postgres only listens on 127.0.0.1.

Unlike hosted finance apps, Meld has no servers: your history and bank connections exist only on your computer, and AI sees only what a question needs.

Meld talks to these outside services only. Each one is optional except Plaid (not used for demo data) and GitHub's update check.

ServiceWhenWhat's sent
Plaid (with your own keys)Linking accounts and every sync (every 6 hours, or on Refresh)Your bank sign-in goes to Plaid and your bank through Plaid's own window, never through Meld. Meld receives balances, transactions, holdings, investment activity and loan details. Tapping Get from bank on an account's estate screen also fetches that checking or savings account's full number (Plaid Auth); it's kept, encrypted, only if you save it.
GitHubMac app update checks (on launch & every 4 hours); What's newNothing about you or your finances: just requests for Meld's public release list (GitHub sees your IP). Updates download only when you click Update.
Yahoo FinanceInvestments → Performance & Equity, and demo dataTicker symbols only, to fetch daily prices.
Anthropic (if you add an API key)Ask Meld with a Claude modelYour question, a compact overview snapshot (totals, counts, basic profile), and whatever the AI looks up to answer (for example matching transactions, holdings or budgets).
Insurance or Equity → Upload PDF / Paste tableThe whole PDF or pasted text you submit.
Home or vehicle value lookupThe property address, or the vehicle's year, make, model, mileage and ZIP code.
Accounts → Investments → ExposureFund tickers and names.
OpenAI (if you sign in with ChatGPT)Ask Meld with a ChatGPT model, on your ChatGPT planThe same as Ask Meld above. Requests are sent with store: false.
OpenRouter (if you add an API key)Ask Meld with an OpenRouter model (Gemini, GPT, Claude and others); without an Anthropic key, also Upload PDF, value lookups and ExposureThe same as for Anthropic above, to OpenRouter and the model's provider (e.g. Google). Requests set data_collection: "deny", so only providers that don't store or train on prompts get them. PDFs go to the model itself, not an OCR service; lookups use the provider's own web search, no third-party engine. See OpenRouter's privacy policy.
Ollama (if you set it up)Ask Meld with a local modelThe same as Ask Meld above, but to Ollama on your own machine or network, so it never leaves your control.
External agents via MCP (off by default; turn on in Settings)When an external agent you configure calls Meld's local MCP endpointRead-only tool and resource responses (with the same digit masking, hidden-account exclusion, and estate contact-detail omission as Ask Meld). Meld sends nothing out itself; whichever AI provider your external agent uses receives what that agent fetches.
Tailscale (if you turn on phone access)While it's onYour phone reaches Meld over your own tailnet, encrypted end to end between your devices. Tailscale's servers coordinate the connection but can't see Meld's traffic. Off by default.
ntfy (if you enter a topic URL under Alerts)Each alert at or above the severity you pick, and Send testThe alert's title and message: account and merchant names, amounts and due dates. Anyone who knows the topic name can read a topic on the public ntfy.sh server, so pick a long random one, or run your own ntfy server to keep alerts on your network. Off unless you set it up; alerts otherwise stay in the app.

Estate planning (Plan → Estate) sends nothing anywhere by itself. The family packet PDF is built on your computer and downloaded to it. It lists your accounts, balances, beneficiaries and contacts, plus any full account and policy numbers you enter, so treat it like a bank statement: give it a password, and keep the printout somewhere safe. With a Claude model, Ask Meld can read beneficiary and document names and roles through get_estate_plan. Phone numbers, emails, full account and policy numbers, your letter and your logins note are never sent.

Before anything goes to an AI model, runs of 9+ digits (account, card, policy and grant numbers) become •••• plus the last 4 digits. Full loan numbers from Plaid are dropped before storage. PDF extraction reads only the last 4 characters; full numbers stay local unless typed in.

Anthropic's API doesn't train on your data by default and keeps it for a limited time for safety reviews; see Anthropic's privacy center for the current policy. If you'd rather nothing goes to a cloud AI, add no Anthropic or OpenRouter key and use Ollama for chat. PDF upload, value lookups and look-through exposure are then turned off.

What's protected on disk#

  • Plaid access tokens, full account and policy numbers kept for the family packet, uploaded insurance, equity and estate PDFs, Plaid, Anthropic and OpenRouter keys saved from the app, and your ChatGPT sign-in are encrypted (AES-256-GCM) with ENCRYPTION_KEY. The Mac app makes that key on first run and keeps it in your macOS Keychain (Windows: Credential Manager); Docker reads it from .env. A copy of the data or documents folder can't be opened without it.
  • The passphrase you choose in the app is stored only as a salted scrypt hash.
  • Everything else (transactions, balances, holdings) is stored unencrypted in the database, protected by your computer's own security. Turn on full-disk encryption (FileVault on macOS, BitLocker on Windows) if you haven't already.
  • With Docker, .env holds your secrets. scripts/setup.sh creates it readable only by you; don't commit or share it.

Reporting a security problem#

Please report it privately through GitHub (the repository's Security tab → Report a vulnerability) rather than in a public issue. SECURITY.md has the details.
